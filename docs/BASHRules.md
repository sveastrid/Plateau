# BASH - Official Game Rules

## 🎯 Objective
Survival is the name of the game. The **last player remaining with an undestroyed piece** is the winner!

---

## 🎮 Controls & Turn Mechanics
* **Action/Attack:** Hold the **Left Trigger** to initiate your piece's attack (either firing a projectile or executing a ramming maneuver).
* **Steer Projectile:** Use the **Right Joystick** to adjust your path mid-flight (whether shooting a missile, ramming, or moving).
* **Defenseless Targets:** There are no defensive mechanics. Opponents remain stationary during your turn while you execute your attack.

---

## ♟️ The Pieces: Abilities & Movement

Each piece has a unique way of moving, attacking, and navigating the map. 

### 🛥️ Boat
* **Turn Sequence:** Fires an arcing shot **first**, and then moves.
* **Combat:** To destroy an opponent, your arcing shot *must* land directly on them. Ramming or bumping into an opponent during your movement phase does nothing.
* **Navigation:** **Cannot** move onto islands. **Cannot** move through or hit walls and bases.

### ✈️ Airplane
* **Turn Sequence:** Fires an arcing shot **first**, and then moves.
* **Combat:** Just like the boat, your arcing shot *must* land directly on an opponent to destroy them. You cannot destroy opponents by crashing into them during movement.
* **Navigation:** **Can** move onto and fly over islands. **Cannot** move through walls or bases.

### ⛴️ Submarine
* **Turn Sequence:** Moves and attacks simultaneously in a single turn. 
* **Combat:** Ramming only! It does not fire projectiles. If your movement path goes directly through an opponent, they are instantly destroyed.
* **Navigation:** **Cannot** move through islands, walls, or bases.

### 🚁 Helicopter
* **Turn Sequence:** Moves and attacks simultaneously in a single turn.
* **Combat:** Ramming only! Like the Submarine, it does not fire projectiles. You must move directly through an opponent to destroy them.
* **Navigation:** **Can** move freely over and through islands. **Cannot** move through walls or bases.
